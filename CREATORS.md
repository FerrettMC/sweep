# Creator outreach

Finding small TikTok and Instagram creators, messaging them, and what to say
when they reply.

---

## Budget reality first

Micro creators (10k to 100k followers) quote **$150 to $800 per video** as of
2026. Opening with "paid promo?" to someone that size gets that number back, and
a free subscription in reply reads as wasting their time.

**Target 1k to 10k followers.** At that size a lot of people have never been
paid for anything, take free product happily, and post because they're excited
rather than contracted. Some will take $20 to $50. That is the tier where a
16 year old with no budget can actually get videos made.

Under 1k is usually not worth the message. Over 10k, expect a rate card.

---

## The opener

Short. The whole point is getting a reply, not explaining the app.

### If you have money for it

> Paid promo? Hey, hope you're doing well. I'm Jude, I'm 16 and I built Sweep, a
> shopping app that searches 5 stores at once and tells you when a "sale" isn't
> real. Would you be down to get a deal going?

Only send this if you will actually pay. "Paid promo?" is why they open it, and
baiting with it burns the one shot you get.

### If you are offering the app instead of cash

Don't fake the paid opener. Lead with the thing that is actually unusual.

> Hey! I'm Jude, I'm 16 and I built a shopping app called Sweep. It searches 5
> stores at once and keeps its own price history, so it can tell when a "40% off"
> is sitting on the normal price.
>
> I want to give you a year of Pro free, no strings, not asking you to post. I
> just think you'd use it and I'd love to know what you think.

Lower reply rate than the paid opener. Better replies, because anyone who
answers actually wants the app.

### Always add one specific line

At the top, reference something they actually posted. Not "love your content."

> your video about the Target clearance tags is what made me check my own store's app

That single line is the whole difference between a reply and a delete. It is
also the reason you cannot paste the same message to everyone.

## When they say "let me know all the details"

This is the message that matters and most people have nothing ready. Have it
written before you send the first DM.

> Sure. Sweep is on the Play Store, it searches Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, eBay
> and Etsy at the same time and keeps its own price history, so it can show when
> a "40% off" badge is sitting on a price that never moved.
>
> What I'm after: one video, your own words, showing a real search. No script, and
> you can say what you don't like about it. I'd rather that than something that
> sounds like an ad.
>
> What I can do: $X, plus a year of Pro for you, plus a code your viewers can use
> for free Pro so it's worth something to them too.
>
> Whatever timeline works for you. Let me know if that's in the right range.

Fill in $X before sending. "Let me know if that's in the right range" invites a
counter instead of a no.

## Finding them

I can't browse TikTok, so this is the method rather than a list.

**Search these terms**, then sort by recent so you skip dead accounts:

- fake sale
- is this deal real
- price tracker
- fake discount
- black friday scam
- how to tell if something is on sale
- amazon price history
- walmart clearance
- target clearance tags

**Then mine the hashtags** on whatever comes up. The good ones are usually
adjacent rather than obvious: deal communities, clearance hunting, couponing,
budget shopping, "no buy" and frugal accounts.

**Also worth searching:** camelcamelcamel, keepa, honey, rakuten. Anyone making
videos about those tools has an audience that already understands what Sweep is,
which removes the hardest part of the pitch.

### What to look for

- 1k to 10k followers
- Posted in the last two weeks. Anything older is a dead account.
- Comments, not just views. 200 views with 20 comments beats 50k views with 3.
- They talk to camera. Faceless repost accounts won't sell anything.
- US based, since that's where the stores are.

### Skip

- Anyone whose bio says "DM for rates" and nothing else. That's a rate card, and
  it will be above budget.
- Accounts posting three times a day. Those are content farms.
- Anyone whose comments are all bots.

## Instagram

Same creators, same video, different mechanics. Worth doing both, since the
vertical file you already cut works on Reels with no changes.

**The DM problem.** A message from someone they don't follow lands in Requests,
which many people check rarely or never. That is the whole game on Instagram.
Ways around it:

- Follow them, watch a few stories, and leave a real comment on a recent post
  first. Then DM. Now you're a name they've seen rather than a request.
- Reply to a story instead of opening a cold DM. Story replies land in the normal
  inbox, not Requests, which is the single biggest trick here.
- If they list an email in their bio, use it. Bio emails get read.

**Rates run a bit higher than TikTok** at the same follower count, roughly 10 to
25% at the nano and micro tiers. Same targeting though: 1k to 10k.

**Where to look:** Reels using the same search terms below, plus the deal and
couponing hashtags. Instagram's search is worse than TikTok's, so hashtags do
more of the work. Check that they post Reels and not only grid photos, since a
static post will do nothing for an app.

**Send fewer.** Instagram is stricter than TikTok about new accounts sending
DMs, and the penalty is a silent block on messaging rather than a warning.

## YouTube

Worth more than TikTok for this app, despite costing more. A TikTok is a spike
that is dead in 48 hours. A YouTube video called "best price tracking apps"
keeps surfacing in search for years, and the people watching it are looking for
exactly this rather than scrolling past it.

**Where the small ones are.** Feedspot keeps ranked lists with subscriber counts
attached, which is what makes them useful for finding the 1k to 50k tier:

- videos.feedspot.com/money_saving_youtube_channels/
- videos.feedspot.com/couponing_youtube_channels/

Work down from the bottom, not the top.

**Named channels found so far**, all need their size checked before pitching:

- Kristie's Connections, around 219k, couponing and Dollar General penny lists
- Karlasavings, couponing and clearance finds
- The Geeky Banana, couponing, glitches and deals
- MoneySavingExpert, large and UK based, so a poor fit while Sweep is US stores

**Finding the business email.** Most channels put it in the About tab behind a
captcha, and plenty just put it in the video description instead. Check the
description first, it is faster and the address is the same.

**Search terms that find the right videos:** best price tracking apps, is this
sale real, black friday scams, camelcamelcamel, keepa, honey alternative. Then
look at who made them rather than at the video.

## The listicle play, which may beat all of this

There is a whole tier of sites publishing "best price tracking apps 2026"
roundups: rategrove, cartlens, hunchdrop, savingsgrove, karmanow and others.
Those articles rank in search, get read by people actively looking for this
exact category, and keep sending installs for years.

Getting Sweep added to one costs an email. Nobody is bidding against you for a
slot, because most of these are written by someone who found ten apps in an
afternoon and would happily have found eleven.

Pitch it as a correction rather than a request: their list is missing an app
that does something none of the others do, which is verify a discount against
its own price history across five stores rather than one.

**Know the competition before you email them**, because they will ask:

- **Karma**, 6.5M users, claims 100,000+ retailers, plus coupons and cashback
- **CamelCamelCamel** and **Keepa**, deep price history, Amazon only
- **Honey**, **Rakuten**, coupons and cashback, no price verification
- **Slickdeals**, **DealNews**, human-curated deal feeds

Sweep's honest position: Camel and Keepa do price history but only for Amazon,
and Karma covers far more stores but does not tell you whether a discount is
real. Doing the history check across five stores in a phone app is the gap.
Claiming more than that gets you caught.

## Two codes, not one

The promo system does any tier for any number of days, with a redemption cap.

- **Creator's own code.** A year of Pro, single use. They have to actually use
  the app before they can talk about it.
- **Their audience's code.** Unique per creator, so you can see exactly whose
  audience converted without having to ask them for stats. ROADMAP has the
  audience code at 14 days of Pro.

## Rules

**Write every message yourself.** TikTok's spam detection kills repeated DMs
from new accounts, and the specific line about their video is what earns the
reply anyway.

**Don't send more than a handful a day** from a new account.

**Never ask for the video before they've used the app.** Someone reading a
script they don't believe makes a worse video than no video.

**Take the honest review.** If a creator says the search is slow, that is free
research and a more credible video than praise.
