# Selling points

Notes for advertising. Everything here is true today — if a claim stops being
true, take it out rather than softening it, because the whole pitch rests on
being the app that doesn't oversell.

---

## The one line

**Sweep finds the real price, and tells you when a sale isn't one.**

Alternatives depending on where it's going:

- _Short (app store, bio):_ Search every store at once. Watch prices. Know when a discount is real.
- _Human (word of mouth):_ It's a shopping app that checks whether the "40% off" is actually 40% off.
- _Blunt (comments, replies):_ Real prices from real stores. No AI guessing, no ads.

---

## The two biggest differentiators

### 1. No AI inventing anything

Your note, sharpened. **Don't use the word "hallucinate" in public copy** — it
invites the reader to wonder whether it happens sometimes. Say what's true
instead:

> **Every price comes from the store itself, not from a model guessing.**

This matters more every month. The shopping assistants launching now are
wrappers around language models, and they confidently return products that
don't exist, prices that were never charged, and links that 404. Sweep reads
the actual listing. If a store won't tell us something, the app says nothing
rather than filling the gap.

Ways to say it:

- "Prices are read, not predicted."
- "If we can't see it, we don't show it."
- "No AI in the price path. There's nothing to make up."

**Proof you can point at:** the product page hides whole sections when a store
doesn't publish them, and says which store doesn't publish what. An app that
was inventing content would never leave a gap on screen.

### 2. Honest about sales, provably

Sweep keeps its own price history, so it can say a red "40% off" badge is
sitting on the price the thing always costs. That's not an opinion or a scrape
of the store's own marketing — it's what Sweep watched happen.

> **The only number on the page a shop can't stage.**

This is the single most quotable feature. Lead with it in anything longer than
a sentence.

## No ads, said properly

Your note said to reword this. The honest version is a strength, not a caveat:

> **No ads. The only one in the app is optional, and it's yours to trigger —
> watch a short ad, get an extra search. That's it.**

Or shorter: **"Nothing interrupts you. The only ad is one you choose to watch."**

Avoid "no ads except…" — leading with the exception makes it sound like a
loophole. Lead with the promise, then the single exception as a *feature* the
user controls.

## The rest, in the order they land

1. **The free tier is genuinely usable.** 5 searches and 12 product lookups a
   day, 3 tracked products, price history, lists, budget. No card, no trial, no
   expiry. Say "free" without an asterisk, because there isn't one.
2. **Real reviews, not a summary we wrote.** What buyers actually said, split
   into what they liked, disliked, and disagree about — including buyer photos.
3. **It watches so you don't.** Track a price, get told when it drops. And if
   notifications are off or you missed one, the bell keeps the record.
4. **Price history you can read at a glance.** A line, not marketing.
5. **Similar products, free.** Cheaper versions of the same thing, from what's
   already been searched.
6. **Made by one person.** Sixteen, no company, no investors, no data to sell.
   This is a real advantage — say it plainly and let people decide if they like
   it. Many will.
7. **"Why are features limited?"** An entire page explaining what each part
   costs to run and what subscriptions pay for. Almost no app does this. It's
   worth a post of its own.

## Words that work

| Use                                  | Instead of                        |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| "read from the store"                | "accurate"                        |
| "when a sale is real"                | "deal finder"                     |
| "it watches for you"                 | "price tracking"                  |
| "what buyers actually said"          | "reviews"                         |
| "nothing interrupts you"             | "ad-free" (you have the one ad)   |
| "one person made this"               | "indie" / "we're a small team"    |

Never say "we" about the team. There's one of you, and saying so is the point.

## Claims to be careful with

Not legal advice, just the ones that could bite.

- **"Checks every hour."** Say **"up to every hour."** Checking cadence depends
  on load and the store answering; promising a frequency you might miss is a
  promise you'd have to keep.
- **Naming stores.** Only name stores that are actually live. The app generates
  its own store lists for this reason; ad copy has no such protection, so it
  goes stale silently. Check before reusing old copy.
- **"Cheapest price."** You compare the stores you cover, which isn't every
  store on earth. Say **"the cheapest of the stores we check"**, or just "we
  check them all at once".
- **"Save up to X%."** Only with a real example you can point at, and only if
  it's typical. An unusual saving as a headline number is the exact behaviour
  Sweep exists to call out.
- **Never frame subscriptions as supporting or funding you.** Google treats
  crowdfunding differently from selling a subscription. Sweep sells a
  subscription — describe what it unlocks, never ask for support.
- **Don't promise the roadmap.** Stores you plan to add are intentions. Say
  "next in line", not "coming soon", and never give a date.

## Angles worth a post each

- **The fake sale.** Screenshot a product where the store's discount badge sits
  on its normal price, next to Sweep's history. This is the demo.
- **"Why are features limited?"** The transparency page itself. Show it, explain
  why you built it. This is the one most likely to travel.
- **Built by a 16-year-old.** The story, honestly told, including what it costs
  to run and what you've learned. Works where a feature list wouldn't.
- **The AI thing.** A short piece on why the price path has no model in it, and
  what goes wrong in the apps that do.
- **What each store will tell you.** Amazon gives reviews, eBay gives seller
  ratings and delivery dates, Etsy gives almost nothing — and Sweep shows
  exactly what each one publishes rather than pretending they're the same.

## Where to say it

- **Play listing** — the short description does the work. Lead with the fake-sale
  line. Long description carries the free-tier detail.
- **Reddit** — r/frugal, r/deals, r/androidapps. These communities hate being
  marketed at, so lead with the transparency page or the 16-year-old story, not
  the feature list. Answer questions honestly, including "what can't it do".
- **TikTok / Shorts** — the fake-sale demo is inherently visual. Fifteen seconds:
  badge says 40% off, Sweep's chart says it's been that price for a month.
- **Friends and family first.** They'll find the bugs, and early real usage is
  what makes the price history worth anything.

---

## What NOT to lead with

- Feature lists. Every shopping app has a feature list.
- "Save money!" — every app says this and nobody believes it.
- The subscription. Nobody has ever installed an app because it has tiers.
- Anything about how it's built. Users don't care that it's Expo and Fastify.
