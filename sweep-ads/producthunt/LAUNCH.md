# Product Hunt launch

## Timing

**Do not launch the day you make the account.** Maker profiles with no history
get filtered, and you get one launch per product. Create it today, spend two
weeks upvoting and commenting on things you actually like, then launch.

Launch at **12:01am PT on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday**. The 24 hours after
that is the whole ranking window, so pick a day you can be at a keyboard.

## Fields

**Name**

    Sweep

**Tagline** (60 char limit, this is what people read on the homepage)

    Search 5 stores at once and check if a sale is real

**Description**

    Sweep searches Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, eBay and Etsy in one go and keeps
    its own price history, so when a store says 40% off it can tell you whether
    that is real or the price it has been all month. Android, free, no ads, and
    no AI anywhere in the price path.

**Links**

    https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sweepshopping.app
    https://sweepshopping.com

**Topics**

    Shopping · Android · E-Commerce

**Gallery** — gallery-1 through gallery-5 in this folder, already 1270x760.
Lead with gallery-2, the fake-sale one. It is the only thing on the list a
competitor cannot copy, and the gallery is most of the first impression.

Add `out/demo.mp4` as the video if the launch form takes one.

## The first comment

Post this yourself, immediately after launching. Around 70% of winners have one,
and an empty first comment reads as a low-effort launch.

---

Hey Product Hunt,

I'm Jude, I'm 16, and I built Sweep because I got annoyed at a "40% off" badge.

The thing that bothers me about online sales is that the same company sets the
original price and sets the discount. You're taking their word for both, and
there's usually no way to check. So Sweep keeps its own price history. If
something has been sitting at the "sale" price for a month, it says so.

The rest grew around that. One search goes out to Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy,
eBay and Etsy at the same time and shows what each is charging. Track a product
and it tells you the moment the price drops. Deal Radar is a standing search, so
instead of picking a listing you say what you're after and a price, and it keeps
looking for weeks.

One decision worth mentioning because it's unusual right now: there is no
language model anywhere in the price path. Every number is read from the actual
listing. When a store doesn't publish something, the app leaves that section
empty and names the store rather than filling the gap. Most shopping assistants
launching this year do the opposite, and confidently return prices nobody was
ever charged.

Getting five stores to answer reliably was about 90% of the work. Two more
parsed perfectly on my machine and died the moment they hit production, which
taught me more than the five that worked.

It's free. 10 searches and 12 product lookups a day, no card, no trial. I'd
really like feedback on the search results screen, I've stared at it so long I
can't see it any more.

---

## On launch day

Reply to every comment, fast. Engagement in the first few hours is the ranking
signal, and a maker who answers is the difference between a launch and a
listing.

Someone will ask whether you used AI to build it. That is a different question
from whether there is AI in the price path, and both answers are fine. Just
don't look evasive.
